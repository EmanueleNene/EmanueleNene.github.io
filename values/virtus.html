Value & Philosophy

Virtus

Moral excellence, valor, and the strength of character to do what is right.

General Meaning

Virtus (from vir, "man") was a specific Roman virtue that carried connotations of valor, manliness, character, and worth. It was not merely about physical courage in battle, but about moral fortitude and the active pursuit of excellence in all things.

For the Romans, Virtus was something to be demonstrated through action. It was the ability to overcome obstacles, whether external or through self-discipline.

What it means for me

Virtus is the proactive side of my philosophy. In research, it translates to the resilience required to fail forward—to keep questioning and experimenting when a path seems blocked. It represents the courage to stay true to the data, even when it’s inconvenient, and to consistently aim for the highest standards of integrity and effort in every endeavor.
